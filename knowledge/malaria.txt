Malaria is a disease caused by parasites spread through the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes, which bite mostly between dusk and dawn. Malaria does not spread directly from person to person.

Malaria symptoms include fever with chills and sweating, headache, body aches, nausea, vomiting and tiredness. Symptoms usually start 10 to 15 days after the bite, and the fever may come in cycles.

Any fever in an area where malaria occurs should be tested. Malaria is diagnosed with a blood test, either a microscope examination or a rapid test.

Severe malaria is an emergency. Signs include confusion, seizures, very pale skin from severe anemia, difficulty breathing, yellow eyes, dark urine and passing very little urine. Untreated falciparum malaria can be fatal.

Malaria treatment uses antimalarial medicines chosen by a doctor depending on the parasite type, and early treatment matters.

Malaria prevention includes sleeping under insecticide-treated bed nets, using mosquito repellent, wearing covered clothing at dusk and dawn, using window screens, and removing standing water. Travellers to malaria-risk areas should ask a doctor about preventive medicines.
