Migraine is a neurological condition that causes repeated moderate to severe headaches, often throbbing on one side of the head. A migraine attack can last from 4 hours to 3 days and often comes with nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light, sound and smell.

Some people have a migraine aura before the headache. Aura symptoms include flashing lights, zigzag lines, blind spots or tingling, and usually last less than an hour.

Common migraine triggers include stress, too little or too much sleep, skipped meals, dehydration, hormonal changes, bright lights, strong smells, weather changes, changes in caffeine intake, and for some people alcohol, chocolate, aged cheese or processed meats. Triggers differ between people, and keeping a headache diary helps find them.

Migraine management during an attack includes resting in a quiet dark room, drinking fluids and using a cold compress. A doctor can recommend pain relief and preventive treatment. Using painkillers too often can itself cause headaches. Regular sleep, meals, hydration, exercise and stress management help prevent migraine.

See a doctor urgently for a sudden severe headache unlike any before, headache with fever and a stiff neck, confusion, weakness, numbness, trouble speaking or seeing, a seizure, headache after a head injury, or a new type of headache after age 50.
