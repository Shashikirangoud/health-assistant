Fever means a body temperature above normal, usually 38 °C (100.4 °F) or higher. Fever is a symptom and not a disease. It often means the body is fighting an infection.

Common causes of fever include viral infections such as colds and flu, bacterial infections, urinary tract infections, and in India especially during the monsoon, dengue, malaria and typhoid. Fever can also follow vaccination or heat exposure.

Home care for fever includes rest, plenty of fluids, light clothing and a comfortable room temperature. Fever medicine for adults or children should follow the product label or a doctor's or pharmacist's advice, because a child's amount depends on weight. Avoid cold baths and alcohol rubs.

See a doctor if a fever lasts more than 3 days or keeps coming back. Seek care sooner if fever comes with a rash, severe headache, stiff neck, confusion, trouble breathing, repeated vomiting, severe stomach pain, or pain while passing urine, or if the person is pregnant, elderly or has a weak immune system or long-term illness.

Fever in children: a baby under 3 months with a fever of 38 °C or higher needs urgent medical assessment. A child with fever should also be seen quickly if drowsy or hard to wake, refusing fluids, showing signs of dehydration, having trouble breathing, or having a seizure.
