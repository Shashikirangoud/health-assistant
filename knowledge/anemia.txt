Anemia (also spelled anaemia) means the body has too few healthy red blood cells or too little haemoglobin, so tissues get less oxygen. Iron deficiency is the most common cause of anemia worldwide, and it is especially common among women, teenage girls, children and pregnant women in India.

Anemia symptoms include tiredness, weakness, pale skin, dizziness, headaches, shortness of breath on effort, a fast heartbeat, cold hands and feet, and brittle nails. Mild anemia may cause few symptoms.

Causes of anemia include too little iron in the diet, blood loss (heavy periods, stomach ulcers, intestinal worms), the extra needs of pregnancy, low vitamin B12 or folate, long-term illness, and inherited blood conditions.

Foods that help with iron deficiency anemia include leafy greens, lentils, beans, chickpeas, jaggery, dates, nuts, seeds, eggs, meat and fish. Eating vitamin C foods such as lemon, amla, oranges or guava with meals helps the body absorb iron. Tea and coffee with meals reduce iron absorption.

Treatment of anemia depends on the cause and starts with a blood test. Iron tablets should be taken only as advised by a doctor, because too much iron is harmful and iron medicine must be kept away from children.

See a doctor if anemia symptoms last, if periods are very heavy, if stools are black, or during pregnancy. Fainting, chest pain or severe breathlessness needs urgent care.
