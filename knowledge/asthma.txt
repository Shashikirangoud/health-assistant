Asthma is a long-term lung condition in which the airways become inflamed and narrow. Asthma causes repeated episodes of wheezing, cough (especially at night or early morning), chest tightness and shortness of breath.

Common asthma triggers include dust mites, pollen, mould, pet dander, smoke from tobacco, firewood or incense, air pollution, cold air, exercise, respiratory infections, strong smells, stress and some medicines. Triggers differ from person to person.

To manage asthma triggers, learn which ones affect you, wash bedding in hot water, keep damp areas dry, avoid smoke, and wear a mask on high-pollution days. Work with a doctor on a written asthma action plan.

Asthma medicines are usually of two kinds: controller (preventer) inhalers used regularly to reduce airway inflammation, and reliever inhalers used during symptoms. Use them exactly as prescribed, ask a doctor or pharmacist to check your inhaler technique, and keep your reliever with you.

Most people with well-controlled asthma can exercise safely. Warming up first helps, and anyone with asthma should follow their doctor's advice about exercise and reliever use.

An asthma attack is an emergency if the person is struggling to breathe, cannot speak full sentences, has blue or grey lips or fingertips, or the reliever inhaler is not helping. Call emergency services immediately.
