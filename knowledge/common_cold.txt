The common cold is a mild viral infection of the nose and throat caused by many different viruses, most often rhinoviruses. Colds spread through droplets from coughs and sneezes, and by touching contaminated surfaces and then the face.

Common cold symptoms include a runny or blocked nose, sneezing, sore throat, cough, mild headache, mild body aches and sometimes a low fever. They usually start 1 to 3 days after exposure.

A common cold usually lasts about 7 to 10 days. A leftover cough can last 2 to 3 weeks. Antibiotics do not work on a cold because colds are caused by viruses.

Home care for a common cold includes rest, plenty of fluids, warm drinks, salt-water gargles and saline nasal drops or spray. Honey can soothe a cough in adults and children over 1 year, but should never be given to babies under 1 year.

See a doctor if cold symptoms last more than 10 days, get worse after improving, or come with high fever, difficulty breathing, wheezing, severe sore throat or ear pain. Babies, and people with asthma, heart or lung disease or weak immunity, should be checked earlier.

Prevent a common cold by washing hands often, covering coughs and sneezes, avoiding touching the face, not sharing towels, and staying home when unwell. Flu usually starts more suddenly than a common cold, with higher fever and stronger body aches.
