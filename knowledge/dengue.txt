Dengue is a viral infection spread by the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes, which bite mainly during the day. It is common in tropical regions including India, and cases rise during and after the rainy season. Dengue does not spread directly from person to person.

Many people with dengue have mild symptoms or none. When symptoms appear, they usually start 4 to 10 days after the bite. Dengue symptoms include high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain, nausea, vomiting, swollen glands and a skin rash.

Severe dengue warning signs usually appear around the time the fever settles, about day 3 to 7. They include severe stomach pain, repeated vomiting, bleeding from the gums or nose, blood in vomit or stool, fast breathing, extreme tiredness or restlessness, and cold clammy skin. A person with these dengue warning signs needs urgent hospital care.

There is no specific antiviral medicine for dengue. Dengue care focuses on rest, drinking plenty of fluids and monitoring by a doctor. Aspirin and ibuprofen-type painkillers can increase bleeding risk in dengue, so a person with suspected dengue should ask a doctor which medicines are safe.

Dengue prevention means stopping mosquito breeding and bites. Empty standing water from coolers, buckets, flower pots and old tyres at least once a week. Use mosquito repellent, wear long sleeves, and use window screens or nets, including during the daytime.
