Typhoid fever, also called enteric fever, is a bacterial infection caused by Salmonella Typhi. Typhoid spreads through food and water contaminated with the faeces of an infected person, so it is common where safe drinking water and sanitation are limited, including parts of South Asia.

Typhoid symptoms usually begin 6 to 30 days after infection. The fever rises gradually over several days. Other typhoid symptoms include weakness, stomach pain, headache, loss of appetite, constipation or diarrhoea, and sometimes a rash of rose-coloured spots.

Typhoid is confirmed with laboratory tests ordered by a doctor, such as a blood culture. Typhoid is treated with antibiotics prescribed by a doctor. Taking antibiotics without advice, or stopping early, contributes to antibiotic resistance.

Serious typhoid complications include bleeding or a hole in the intestine. Severe stomach pain, blood in the stool, confusion or collapse during typhoid need emergency hospital care.

Typhoid prevention: drink safe boiled or treated water, wash hands with soap before eating and after using the toilet, eat freshly cooked hot food, peel fruit yourself, and avoid raw street food and ice of uncertain source. A typhoid vaccine is available, and a doctor can advise who should have it.
