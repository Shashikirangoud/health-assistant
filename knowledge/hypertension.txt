Hypertension, or high blood pressure, means the force of blood against the artery walls stays too high. It often causes no symptoms, which is why it is called a silent condition and is found only by measuring blood pressure.

High blood pressure is usually diagnosed when repeated readings are 140/90 mmHg or higher, although some guidelines use lower targets. One high reading does not confirm hypertension, so doctors look at repeated measurements.

Risk factors for high blood pressure include eating too much salt, being overweight, little physical activity, tobacco use, heavy alcohol use, stress, family history and older age.

To lower high blood pressure, the main lifestyle steps are eating less salt (under about 5 grams a day), eating more fruits and vegetables, being active most days, reaching a healthy weight, quitting tobacco and limiting alcohol.

People prescribed blood pressure medicine should keep taking it even when they feel fine, and should not stop it without asking their doctor. Uncontrolled hypertension raises the risk of heart attack, stroke, heart failure and kidney disease.

Seek emergency care if a very high blood pressure reading comes with severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, vision changes, confusion or weakness on one side of the body.
