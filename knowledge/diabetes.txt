Diabetes is a long-term condition in which the body cannot control blood sugar (glucose) properly, either because it does not make enough insulin or cannot use insulin well. The main types are type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes and gestational diabetes, which appears during pregnancy.

Type 2 diabetes is the most common type. It is linked to excess body weight, low physical activity, family history and age. Type 1 diabetes usually starts earlier in life, and the body stops making insulin, so people with type 1 diabetes need insulin treatment.

Common diabetes symptoms include increased thirst, frequent urination, constant hunger, tiredness, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, unexplained weight loss, and tingling in the hands or feet. Many people with type 2 diabetes have no symptoms for years, so regular screening matters.

Diabetes is diagnosed with blood tests such as fasting glucose and HbA1c, ordered by a doctor. A single reading on a home glucose meter cannot confirm diabetes.

Diabetes management includes healthy eating with fewer sugary drinks and refined carbohydrates, regular physical activity, a healthy weight, regular check-ups, and medicines or insulin exactly as prescribed. People with diabetes should not stop or change their medicines without asking their doctor.

Over time, uncontrolled diabetes can damage the heart, kidneys, eyes, nerves and feet. Seek urgent care for very high blood sugar with vomiting, deep fast breathing, confusion or drowsiness, and for low blood sugar with shaking, sweating or confusion that does not improve.
